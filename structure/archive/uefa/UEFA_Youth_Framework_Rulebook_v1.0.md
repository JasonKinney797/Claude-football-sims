# Rulebook: UEFA Youth Framework

| | |
|---|---|
| **Version** | **1.0 (Step 1: national structures)** |
| **Scope** | Boys' youth league football in **all 55 UEFA member associations**, plus the clubs that play inside another association's system (Chapter 7) |
| **Built from** | **Voetbal Vlaanderen Youth Rulebook v1.5** (the model) and **England Youth Rulebook v1.0**, which becomes **Annex ENG (v1.1)** of this rulebook |
| **Accepted by** | UEFA and all 55 member associations, with their leagues and regional bodies |
| **Not in this version** | The three continental competitions (**Step 2**). Decisions already taken for Step 2 are recorded in Chapter 9 |

---

## Chapter 1: How this rulebook works

### Article 1.1: Three layers
1. **The Framework** (Chapters 2 to 8): the rules every association must follow.
2. **The national annex**: each association's own structure inside the Framework (levels, geography, table sizes, licence, formats below U14).
3. **Existing national rules**: everything the Framework and the annex do not change stays in force (Article 8.3).

### Article 1.2: Annexes in this version
| Annex | Status |
|---|---|
| **Annex BEL** | **Voetbal Vlaanderen Youth Rulebook v1.5**, in force, plus the FFA's equivalent rules for the francophone wing |
| **Annex ENG** | **England Youth Rulebook v1.1**, printed in full at the end of this rulebook |
| **The other 53 associations** | **Outline annexes** in Chapter 6: band, Elite format, professional divisions and geography. Each is written out in full **when it is first simulated** |

### Article 1.3: Bands
Every association is placed in one of **four bands** by size, player numbers and geography (Chapter 6). The band sets the **default Elite format**. An annex may only depart from its band's default where this rulebook says so.

## Chapter 2: Common rules for every association

### Article 2.1: Age groups
1. Every age group is **one birth year**.
2. **Exception, Band D:** age groups are **pairs of birth years**: U12–13, U14–15, U16–17 and U18–19. Younger players may always play up.

### Article 2.2: Cohort principle
A cohort is a club's team in one age group. Each season it **moves up one age group**, and **the level belongs to the cohort, not to the age group**. This applies in every association.

### Article 2.3: Standings
Every association keeps standings **from U12 at the latest**. An annex may start earlier (Belgium: U10).

### Article 2.4: Last age group
An association's leagues run **at least up to U18**. Annex BEL runs to U19; Annex ENG runs to U18, with U21 football unchanged.

### Article 2.5: Match formats
1. **11v11 from U14** in every association.
2. **Below U14** the annex chooses the format. Annex BEL plays 8v8 up to U13; Annex ENG plays 9v9 at U12–U13.

### Article 2.6: Points and ranking
**Win 3, draw 1, loss 0.** Each annex sets its tiebreakers. **Points per match** is used whenever tables of different sizes are compared, in every association and across associations.

### Article 2.7: One full-season league
Every level plays **one full-season series**. There are no autumn or spring series.

---

## Chapter 3: The Elite level

### Article 3.1: Every association has an Elite
Every association runs a **national Elite level for every age group from U13** to its last age group. It is the top level of the national youth structure.

### Article 3.2: Default Elite format per band

| Band | Elite 1 | Elite 2 | Levels below Elite (default) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | One **national** table | **Regional** tables (two or three groups) | **Four**: Interregional, Regional, District, Local |
| **B** | One **national** table | One **national** table | **Three**: Interregional, Regional, Local (the Belgian model) |
| **C** | One **national** table, **no Elite 2** | — | **Three**: National 2, Regional, Local |
| **D** | One **national league** per paired age group | — | None, or one level where player numbers allow |

1. Table sizes are set per association in Chapter 6.
2. An annex may **rename** the levels (Belgium: Interprovinciaal, Provinciaal, Gewestelijk) but keeps their **rank**.

### Article 3.3: Reaching Elite: on the pitch
1. **Default for every association:** Elite is reached **on the pitch**, as in Annex BEL v1.5. The licence is a condition for playing Elite, not a way in, and becoming professional gives no Elite place.
2. **Exception: Annex ENG** keeps the **licence track** of England v1.0: licensed academies play the Academy Leagues, everyone else plays the grassroots pyramid, and there is no promotion between the two (Annex ENG Art. 1.4 and 5.7).
3. **Band D** has no promotion into Elite, since it has one national league.

### Article 3.4: Promotion and relegation around Elite (default)
1. **Elite 1 ↔ Elite 2:** the bottom two of Elite 1 go down, and the top two of Elite 2 go up. In Band A with regional Elite 2 groups, **each group winner goes up**, and Elite 1 relegates as many teams as there are groups.
2. **Elite 2 (or Band C Elite) ↔ the level below:** each table winner of the level below goes up, if licensed. Elite relegates **as many as come up**.
3. **Below target:** an Elite table that is smaller than its target relegates nobody until it reaches it (Annex BEL Art. 4.8.4).
4. **Passing down, declining and vacancies** follow Annex BEL Articles 4.8.3, 4.10 and 4.11.

### Article 3.5: Elite licence (default)
Every Elite team needs a licence. Unless an annex sets its own, the **two-tier criteria of Annex BEL Chapter 5** apply: full criteria for professional clubs and for any club with 4+ Elite teams, and lighter criteria for a non-professional club with 1–3 Elite teams. Each association's existing licensing body runs the audit.

## Chapter 4: Professional clubs and second teams

### Article 4.1: Professional club
1. A **professional club** is a club whose senior first team plays, **in the current season**, in one of the divisions its annex lists as **professional divisions** (Chapter 6).
2. Status is fixed **on the first day of the season**, and is checked per club registration number, covering every entity its youth teams play under (Annex BEL Art. 1.9).
3. For **Band C**, the professional division is the **top division**, whether or not every club in it is fully professional. **Band D** has no professional clubs.

### Article 4.2: One team per age group
A professional club fields **exactly one team per age group**, at whatever level each cohort plays. It has **no second or further teams**.

### Article 4.3: Second teams of other clubs
1. A second team starts **two levels below its club's first team** in the same age group, never below the lowest level.
2. Two teams of the same club are **never in the same table**.
3. **No second team beside an Elite team**, and a second team can **never** reach Elite (Annex BEL Art. 4.12.4 and 4.12.7).
4. An annex sets how far a second team may climb below Elite (Annex BEL: up to its first team's level; Annex ENG: one level below its first team).

### Article 4.4: No feeder Elite teams
A player who has played for a professional club's team in a season cannot play for another club's Elite team that season. A player cannot play for two clubs' Elite teams in one season. No in-season loans into or out of an Elite team.

## Chapter 5: Travel and calendar

### Article 5.1: Domestic travel
Each annex sets its domestic travel rules. **Annex BEL:** the RBFA pays every trip of 50 km or more for non-professional clubs, and every Youth Beker trip from round 3. **Annex ENG:** licensed academies use their own transport (Annex ENG Art. 5.1).

### Article 5.2: Professional clubs pay their own way
Where an annex has federation travel, **professional clubs' U13 and older teams are excluded** from it.

### Article 5.3: Continental travel
Set in **Step 2**.

### Article 5.4: Match days
Each annex sets its league match day (Annex BEL: Saturday; Annex ENG: Sunday for grassroots, Saturday for academies). **Tuesdays and Wednesdays in the continental windows** (Step 2) stay free of national league matches for teams still in a continental competition.

## Chapter 7: Clubs playing in another association's system

### Article 7.1: Rule
A club whose senior team plays in **another association's league system** plays its youth teams **in that system**, under **that association's annex**, and qualifies for the continental competitions **through that association**.

### Article 7.2: Known cases

| Club(s) | Own association | Plays and qualifies through |
|---|---|---|
| **Cardiff City, Swansea City, Wrexham, Newport County** (and other Welsh clubs in the English pyramid) | Wales | **England** (Annex ENG) |
| **Derry City** | Northern Ireland | **Republic of Ireland** |
| **Berwick Rangers** | England | **Scotland** |
| **AS Monaco** | Monaco (not a UEFA member) | **France** |
| **FC Andorra** | Andorra | **Spain** |
| **Liechtenstein's clubs** (for example FC Vaduz) | Liechtenstein | **Switzerland** |

### Article 7.3: Placement
1. For geography, the club is placed in **the nearest region** of the association it plays in.
2. A club can only change association between seasons, and only if its senior team changes league system.

|---|---|---|---|

2. The **first continental season** is **2030-31**, using the 2029-30 national tables (cohort principle). The timeline for Step 2 can still change.

### Article 8.2: Seeding (the other 53)
Each association seeds its first season **by the level its cohorts actually played in 2028-29**, as Annex BEL Chapter 9 did: Elite places go to the clubs whose academies played the real top youth competitions, and the rest is filled by ranking. Seeding is one-off.

### Article 8.3: Everything else
All UEFA, FIFA and national rules **not changed by this Framework or an annex stay in force**, including registration, discipline, safeguarding and the rules on international transfers of minors (FIFA Regulations on the Status and Transfer of Players, Article 19).

## Appendix B: research notes

- **UEFA has 55 member associations.** Monaco is not one: AS Monaco is affiliated with the French federation.
- **Russia** has been suspended from UEFA and FIFA competitions since February 2022 in reality; in this universe it is a full member.
- **Liechtenstein** has no national league: its clubs play in the Swiss system. FC Vaduz plays in the Swiss professional leagues and qualifies for Europe through the Liechtenstein Cup in reality.
- **Welsh clubs in England:** Cardiff City, Swansea City, Wrexham and Newport County play in the English pyramid. **Derry City** plays in the League of Ireland; **Berwick Rangers** in the Scottish system; **FC Andorra** in the Spanish system.
- **The real UEFA Youth League** is U19 only, with a Champions League path and a domestic champions path. In this universe it is replaced by the U19 Youth Champions League (Step 2).
- **Regional bodies** named in Chapter 6 (Germany's 21 Landesverbände, Spain's 19 territorial federations, Portugal's 22 district associations, Poland's 16 voivodeship associations) are the real bodies. Their exact grouping into levels is written in each full annex.
- **Not yet verified:** the current official names of some second divisions after rebrands (for example Russia's First League, formerly FNL). They are checked when each annex is written in full.

### Chapter 1: Definitions

#### Article 1.1: Age groups
Every age group is **one birth year**, from **U12 to U18**. **U18 is the last age group** in this rulebook.

#### Article 1.2: Team and cohort
A **team** is a club's side in one age group. Each season the team **moves up one age group** with its players (this season's U12 is next season's U13). **A level belongs to the team, not to the age group.**

#### Article 1.3: Formats and match length

| Age groups | Format | Match length |
|---|---|---|
| U12–U13 | **9v9** | 2 × 30 minutes |
| U14–U15 | **11v11** | 2 × 35 minutes |
| U16 | 11v11 | 2 × 40 minutes |
| U17–U18 | 11v11 | 2 × 45 minutes |

#### Article 1.4: Two tracks
1. **The grassroots pyramid** (Chapter 3): every club without an Academy Licence.
2. **The Academy Leagues** (Chapter 5): every club with an Academy Licence.

A club is in **exactly one track** for all its youth teams.

#### Article 1.5: Standings
**Every league in this rulebook keeps standings**, from U12. **Win 3, draw 1, loss 0.** Ranking order: points, wins, goal difference, goals scored, head-to-head points, head-to-head goal difference. **Points per match** is used whenever tables of different sizes are compared.

---

### Chapter 2: Geography

#### Article 2.1: Regions
England is divided into the **9 official regions of England** (the former government office regions, as used by the Office for National Statistics). Their borders are fixed by this rulebook as listed in the Appendix and **do not change** if the government changes its own regions.

#### Article 2.2: Halves and areas
1. Every region is split into **2 halves**. Each half is used for one **Championship** league (Level 2).
2. Every half is split into **2 areas**. Each area is used for one **League One** league (Level 3) and its District tables (Level 4).
3. Every region therefore has **2 halves and 4 areas**. England has **18 halves and 36 areas**.
4. Halves and areas are built from **whole local authorities** (counties, unitary authorities, metropolitan boroughs and London boroughs). The full list is in the Appendix.

#### Article 2.3: Where a club belongs
A club belongs to the area of **its registered home ground**. A club can change its registered home ground only between seasons.

---

### Chapter 3: The grassroots pyramid

#### Article 3.1: Levels
For **every age group from U12 to U18**:

| Level | Name | Leagues | Per | Teams per league |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Regional Premier** | **9** | 1 per region | **14** |
| **2** | **Regional Championship** | **18** | 1 per half | **14** |
| **3** | **Regional League One** | **36** | 1 per area | **14** |
| **4** | **District** | **Variable** (Art. 3.3) | Under each League One | **At most 14** |

#### Article 3.2: Fixed counts

| | Per age group | All 7 age groups |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed leagues (Levels 1–3)** | **63** | **441** |
| **Teams at Levels 1–3** | **882** | **6,174** |

#### Article 3.3: District tables
1. Every League One area has **its own District tables**. Every District table belongs to exactly one area.
2. The number of District tables in an area is **the number of District entries divided by 14, rounded up**. Teams are spread as evenly as possible.
3. District tables are drawn **by distance**: the FA groups the teams with the smallest average distance between registered home grounds.
4. **The FA publishes every league, every table and every team by 1 August** each year.

#### Article 3.4: Names
Every league is named by **age group, region and level**, for example:
- **U14 North West Premier**
- **U14 North West Championship North**
- **U14 North West League One C**
- **U14 North West League One C – District 3**

#### Article 3.5: Second and further teams
1. A club's **second team** starts **two levels below its first team** in the same age group, never below Level 4.
2. **Third and further teams** start at Level 4. A club may enter as many Level 4 teams as it wants.
3. Two teams of the same club are **never in the same league**.
4. A second team can be promoted **up to one level below its first team**. If it finishes above its first team, the designations swap at the end of the season.

---

### Chapter 4: Season, promotion and relegation (grassroots)

#### Article 4.1: Season
1. One full-season league: **double round robin, 26 matchdays**, from the **first Sunday of September** to the **last Sunday of April**.
2. Grassroots league matches are played on **Sundays**.
3. **Play-off window:** the 3 Sundays after the last matchday.

#### Article 4.2: Level 1 ↔ Level 2 (per region)
1. **Both Championship champions are promoted** to the Regional Premier.
2. **The bottom 2 of the Regional Premier are relegated** to the Championship of **their own half**.

#### Article 4.3: Level 2 ↔ Level 3 (per half)
1. **Both League One champions** of the half's two areas are **promoted** to that half's Championship.
2. The Championship then relegates **as many teams as needed to return to 14** next season, with **a minimum of 2**. The relegated teams go to the League One of **their own area**.

#### Article 4.4: Level 3 ↔ Level 4 (per area)
1. The **champions of all District tables** in an area play the **District play-offs**: single knockout, at the higher-ranked team's ground, byes for the best champions by points per match when needed. **The two finalists are promoted.**
2. League One then relegates **as many teams as needed to return to 14**, with **a minimum of 2**. Relegated teams go to the District tables of their own area.

#### Article 4.5: Play-off draws
Up to U15: straight to penalties. From U16: 2 × 10 minutes of extra time, then penalties.

#### Article 4.6: Entry at U12
A club's new U12 **starts at the level the club's U13 plays that same season**. A club with no U13 starts at **Level 4**.

#### Article 4.7: Last age group
Promotion and relegation earned at **U18 lapses**.

#### Article 4.8: Vacancies
A club that cannot field a team next season **loses its place**. The place goes to the best-placed team that missed promotion from the level below, then to the best-placed relegated team.

#### Article 4.9: Accepting promotion
A club accepts promotion **in writing within 7 days** of the end of the play-off window. **No answer counts as a refusal.** A refused place passes under Art. 4.8.

### Chapter 5: The Academy Leagues

#### Article 5.1: Academy Licence
The **Academy Licence replaces EPPP Categories 1–4.** It is **pass or fail**, checked every season by the Premier League and the EFL together. Decisions are published **by 30 April**.

| Criterion | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Sporting** | The club's senior first team plays in the **Premier League or the EFL** |
| **Pitches** | At least **2 full-size natural grass pitches and 1 full-size artificial pitch**, used exclusively by the academy, owned or leased for a full season |
| **Indoor** | An indoor training area large enough for a 9v9 session |
| **Staff** | An academy manager; **one full-time lead coach per age group (U12–U18)**; at least 2 full-time physiotherapists; a sports scientist; an education and welfare officer; a safeguarding officer |
| **Medical** | A doctor reachable on every match day |
| **Education** | A partnership with at least one school or college for players from U16 |
| **Transport** | Own transport for every age group to away matches |

#### Article 5.2: One team per age group
A licensed club fields **exactly one team per age group, U12 to U18**. It has **no second teams** and **no teams in the grassroots pyramid**.

#### Article 5.3: Two tiers
For **every age group from U12 to U18**:

| Tier | Teams | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **Academy League 1 (AL1)** | **20** | Double round robin, **38 matchdays** |
| **Academy League 2 (AL2)** | **All other licensed clubs** | With 24 teams or fewer: one national table, double round robin. With more than 24: **AL2 North and AL2 South**, as equal in size as possible, split by the regions in Art. 5.4 |

#### Article 5.4: AL2 North and South
- **North:** North East, North West, Yorkshire and the Humber, East Midlands, West Midlands
- **South:** East of England, London, South East, South West

If one half would have more than 4 teams more than the other, the FA moves clubs from the **border regions (East Midlands and West Midlands)** to the smaller half, closest clubs first.

**Welsh clubs** (Framework Chapter 7) *(new in v1.1)*: **Wrexham** is placed in **North**; **Cardiff City, Swansea City and Newport County** in **South**. Any other licensed Welsh club goes to the half whose nearest English region is closer to its registered home ground.

#### Article 5.5: Promotion and relegation (academies)
1. **The bottom 3 of AL1 are relegated** to AL2.
2. **Into AL1:**
   - **One AL2 table:** the **top 3 are promoted**.
   - **AL2 North and South:** **both champions are promoted**, plus **the winner of a play-off** between the two runners-up (one match, at the ground of the runner-up with more points per match).
3. The same **team principle** applies: the level belongs to the team (Art. 1.2). A new U12 starts at the level of the club's U13 (as Art. 4.6). Movement earned at U18 lapses.

#### Article 5.6: Match day
Academy League matches are played on **Saturdays**.

#### Article 5.7: Losing or gaining the licence
1. **A club that loses the licence** (for example, through relegation out of the EFL) enters **all its youth teams into the grassroots pyramid** the next season, at **Level 1 (Regional Premier) of its region**, as **extra teams**. The Regional Premier relegates the same number of extra teams that season, so tables are back at 14 a year later.
2. **A club that gains the licence** enters **all its youth teams into AL2** the next season. Its places in the grassroots pyramid go to the next teams in line (Art. 4.8).

### Chapter 6: Recruitment and compensation (licensed academies)

#### Article 6.1: The 90-minute rule
Up to and including **U14**, a licensed academy may only register a player who lives **within 90 minutes' travel** of the academy's main training ground, by car, in normal traffic, as calculated by the FA's standard route planner.

#### Article 6.2: From U15
From **U15**, **no distance limit** applies.

#### Article 6.3: Compensation
When a player moves from one licensed academy to another, the new club pays the old club **a fixed amount for every season the player was registered there**:

| Seasons registered at | Amount per season |
|---|---|
| U9–U11 | **£3,000** |
| U12–U16 | **£10,000** |
| U17–U18 | **£20,000** |

The same table applies to every licensed club. There are no category multipliers.

#### Article 6.4: Registration age
Licensed academies may register players **from U9**. U9–U11 play no leagues (Art. 6.5).

#### Article 6.5: U9–U11 in academies
Academies' U9–U11 play **festivals and fixtures without standings**, in line with the FA's grassroots rule for those ages.

---

### Chapter 7: The FA Youth Cups

#### Article 7.1: One cup per age group
**One FA Youth Cup per age group, from U13 to U18** (6 cups). The existing FA Youth Cup becomes the **U18 FA Youth Cup**.

#### Article 7.2: Entry

| Round | Who enters |
|---|---|
| **Round 1** | Every **Level 2** team (Regional Championship) |
| **Round 2** | Every **Level 1** team (Regional Premier) |
| **Round 3** | Every **AL2** team |
| **Round 4** | Every **AL1** team; **straight knockout to the final** from here |

1. **Only first teams** take part. Levels 3 and 4 do not enter.
2. Entry is based on **the level the team plays that season**.
3. **Byes are given in Rounds 1 to 3** so that **exactly 64 teams play Round 4**. Byes go to the teams **entering in that round** with the best points per match from the previous season.

#### Article 7.3: Home team
**The team from the lower level plays at home.** When both play at the same level, the first team drawn plays at home.

#### Article 7.4: Drawn matches
U13–U15: straight to penalties. U16–U18: 2 × 10 minutes of extra time, then penalties.

#### Article 7.5: Dates and final
1. Rounds 1 to 3: three fixed cup weekends in **October, November and December**. Round 4 onwards: fixed weekends from **January**.
2. Matches are played on **the home team's normal match day** of the cup weekend (Sunday for grassroots, Saturday for academies).
3. **All six finals** are played at **St George's Park**, Burton upon Trent, on **one weekend in May**.

### Chapter 8: Launch

#### Article 8.1: Timeline

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adoption** | 2026-27 |
| **Structuring season** | **2026-27**: played under the current system; used to audit licences, draw the leagues and publish everything |
| **First season** | **2027-28** |

#### Article 8.2: Academy Licence at launch
Every club whose senior team plays in the Premier League or the EFL in 2026-27 may apply. Licences for 2027-28 are published by **30 April 2027**.

#### Article 8.3: Academy League seeding
1. **AL1** at launch: the licensed clubs that held **EPPP Category 1** in 2026-27, ranked by their **2026-27 U18 Premier League points per match**. **The top 20 enter AL1** in every age group.
2. **AL2:** every other licensed club.

#### Article 8.4: Grassroots seeding (one time only)
For every age group, by the level of the club's **senior first team in 2026-27**:

| Senior team in 2026-27 | Youth teams start at |
|---|---|
| Unlicensed EFL club | **Level 1** (Regional Premier) |
| National League System **Steps 1–2** | **Level 1** |
| **Steps 3–4** | **Level 2** |
| **Steps 5–6** | **Level 3** |
| **Step 7 and below**, and **youth-only clubs** | **Level 4** |

1. **Youth-only clubs** and clubs from Step 7 down may **apply to start one or two levels higher**, based on their teams' **2025-26 and 2026-27 league records**. The FA decides by **1 June 2027**.
2. **When a level has more teams than places**, places go by the 2026-27 league records (points per match). Teams that don't fit drop one level.
3. **When a level has fewer teams than places**, it is filled from the level below in the same order.

#### Article 8.5: Seeding is one-off
From 2028-29, **only results** (Chapters 4 and 5) and **the licence** (Art. 5.7) move teams. A senior team's promotion or relegation has **no effect** on its youth teams.

### Chapter 9: Everything else

#### Article 9.1
All FA, Premier League and EFL regulations **not changed by this rulebook or by the UEFA Youth Framework stay in force**, including U21 competitions, registrations, discipline, refereeing, safeguarding and the rules on international transfers of minors.
