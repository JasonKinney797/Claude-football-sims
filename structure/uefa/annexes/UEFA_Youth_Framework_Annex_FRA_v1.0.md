# Annex FRA: France Youth Competition Structure (v1.0)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Version** | **1.0**, written for the first French campaign. Part of the **UEFA Youth Framework v1.3** |
| **Body** | **Fédération Française de Football (FFF)**, with its **13 metropolitan regional leagues**, their districts, and the overseas leagues |
| **Band** | **A** (Framework Art. 6.1) |
| **Professional divisions** | **Ligue 1 and Ligue 2** (Framework Art. 4.1). National and below are not professional |
| **Model** | Annex BEL v1.5. Everything this annex doesn't change follows Annex BEL, then the Framework, then existing FFF rules (Framework Art. 8.3) |

---

## Chapter 1: Age groups, formats and standings

### Article 1.1: Age groups
One birth year per age group (Framework Art. 2.1), **U6 to U19**.

### Article 1.2: Formats

| Age groups | Format |
|---|---|
| **U6–U13** | **8v8** |
| **U14–U19** | **11v11** |

U13 also plays 8v8 in Europe (Framework Art. 16.1).

### Article 1.3: Standings
1. **Standings from U9.** U6–U8 play without standings: plateaux and friendly festivals, as in the FFF's current *football d'animation*.
2. From U9: win 3, draw 1, loss 0; tiebreakers as Annex BEL.

### Article 1.4: Playing time
From U6 to U13, every player on the match sheet plays at least half of the match (Annex BEL Art. 4.13.2).

## Chapter 2: The levels

### Article 2.1: U13 to U19

| Rank | Level | Tables | Teams |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Elite 1** | **One national table** | **18** |
| 2 | **Elite 2** | **North** and **South** | **16 each** |
| 3 | **Interregional** | **8 groups** (below) | 14 each (13–15 allowed) |
| 4 | **Regional** | **R1** of each of the 13 metropolitan leagues | 12–14 each |
| 5 | **District** | The top division of each district | Set by the district |
| 6 | **Local** | Every division below | Set by the district |

### Article 2.2: U9 to U12
No Elite. **Interregional is the top level**, then Regional, District and Local, in the same groups as Article 2.3.

### Article 2.3: The eight Interregional groups

| Group | Regional leagues | Elite 2 group it feeds |
|---|---|---|
| **N1** | Hauts-de-France, Normandie | North |
| **N2** | Paris Île-de-France | North |
| **N3** | Grand Est, Bourgogne-Franche-Comté | North |
| **N4** | Bretagne, Pays de la Loire, Centre-Val de Loire | North |
| **S1** | Nouvelle-Aquitaine | South |
| **S2** | Occitanie | South |
| **S3** | Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes | South |
| **S4** | Méditerranée, Corse | South |

Group sizes may move between **13 and 15** from season to season. The FFF redraws the boundaries only if a group would leave that range.

## Chapter 3: Promotion and relegation

| Between | Up | Down |
|---|---|---|
| **Elite 1 ↔ Elite 2** | The **winners of Elite 2 North and South** | The **bottom two** of Elite 1 |
| **Elite 2 ↔ Interregional** | The **winner of each Interregional group** (licensed): four into each Elite 2 group | The **bottom four** of each Elite 2 group, into the Interregional group of their league |
| **Interregional ↔ Regional** | **Each R1 champion** (13) | Each Interregional group relegates **as many as its leagues send up** (N1 2, N2 1, N3 2, N4 3, S1 1, S2 1, S3 1, S4 2) |
| **Regional and below** | Set by each regional league and district | |

1. **The cohort principle** (Framework Art. 2.2): a U12 team that wins its Interregional group plays **Elite 2 as U13**.
2. **Below target, passing down, declining and vacancies:** Annex BEL Art. 4.8–4.11.
3. **Promotion earned at U19 lapses** (Annex BEL Art. 4.3).

---

## Chapter 4: Professional clubs

1. **U9 to U12:** a professional club's teams **take Interregional places by right**, placed before any ranking and never relegated (Annex BEL Art. 1.4.3). Its U6–U8 play locally.
2. **Entry at U13:** as Annex BEL Art. 4.2a. A professional club's new U13 enters at **the higher** of the level its outgoing U13 played and the level its U12 earned. A non-professional club's U13 enters only at the level it earned.
3. **AS Monaco** is a French club for every purpose of this annex (Framework Art. 7.2).

---

## Chapter 5: Second teams and licence

1. **Second teams:** two levels below their first team, never in the same table, never in Elite and never beside an Elite team (Framework Art. 4.3). Here they can climb to **one level below their first team**.
2. **Licence:** the two-tier criteria of Annex BEL Chapter 5, audited by the FFF's technical department (DTN). The licence is a condition for playing Elite, never a way in.

---

## Chapter 6: Calendar and match days

| | |
|---|---|
| **League match day** | **Saturday** |
| **Backup** | **Sunday**, when a Saturday can't be used (pitch, weather, a club's clash) |
| **Emergency** | **Wednesday**, for matches that can't be played on either day. Most French schools have no lessons on Wednesday afternoons |
| **Continental matches** | No national league match the day before, the day of, or the day after (Framework Art. 14.3); a moved match goes to the next free Saturday, Sunday or Wednesday |

---

## Chapter 7: Travel

| Who | What the FFF pays |
|---|---|
| **Non-professional clubs, all teams** | Every league trip of **150 km or more** one way, at every level. **Every flight** (Corsica, the overseas leagues), with one night's hotel |
| **Non-professional clubs, Coupe de France** | Every trip **from the round Elite 2 enters** (round 3), at any distance |
| **Professional clubs' U13–U19** | **Nothing**, in any competition (Framework Art. 5.2) |
| **Professional clubs' U6–U12** | Covered like any other club |

**Why 150 km:** France is about eighteen times the size of Belgium. At Belgium's 50 km, the FFF would be paying for almost every Interregional match.

## Chapter 8: The Coupe de France des jeunes

### Article 8.1: One cup per age group, U12 to U19
Eight competitions per season. **The U18 cup is the Coupe Gambardella** and keeps its name.

### Article 8.2: Who enters, and when

| Round | Who enters |
|---|---|
| **Round 1** | **Regional** teams |
| **Round 2** | **Interregional** teams, and **the overseas champions** (Article 8.4) |
| **Round 3** | **Elite 2** teams |
| **Round 4** | **Elite 1** teams |

1. U12 has no Elite: U12 has three entry rounds (Regional, then Interregional and the overseas champions, then the round of 32).
2. **District and Local teams don't enter.**
3. **Numbers:** the FFF gives byes in Round 1 so that every later round has an even number of teams. From Round 4, the cup is a straight knockout to the final.
4. **Only first teams** take part (Annex BEL Art. 4.12.6).
5. **Drawn matches:** up to U15, straight to penalties; from U16, extra time and then penalties (Annex BEL Art. 7.5).

### Article 8.3: Finals
1. **U12–U17 and U19:** a finals weekend at the **Centre National du Football, Clairefontaine**, in May.
2. **U18 (Coupe Gambardella):** at the **Stade de France**, before the senior Coupe de France final, as it is played now.

### Article 8.4: The overseas leagues
1. **Guadeloupe, Martinique, Guyane, La Réunion, Mayotte and Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon** keep their own league structures, run by their leagues.
2. **Each overseas league's champion**, in every age group from U12 to U19, enters the Coupe de France in **Round 2**.
3. Overseas teams play every tie **in mainland France**, at the opponent's ground. The FFF pays flights and hotel for the squad and four staff.
4. **The route to Europe:** an overseas team that wins its Coupe de France takes the **cup place** (Framework Art. 11.3) like any other winner.

### Article 8.5: The cup place in Europe
The winner of a cohort's Coupe de France takes that cohort's cup place next season. **The U12 cup gives the YEL U13 place**, **the U18 Coupe Gambardella the YEL U19 place**. If the winner has already qualified through the league, the place passes down (Framework Art. 11.3).

## Chapter 9: U13 qualification for Europe

**The problem:** France's Elite starts at U13, so a U13 cohort's previous season (U12) has no national table. Ranking 8 Interregional groups by points per match would treat a group with three professional clubs the same as a group with none.

### Article 9.1: The Phase finale nationale U12
1. Every June, after the league season, the **8 Interregional U12 group winners and 8 runners-up** play a national final phase at **Clairefontaine**, 8v8.
2. **Format:** four groups of four (one match a day, three days), then quarter-finals, semi-finals, a third-place match and the final.
3. **Ranking:** 1st and 2nd are the finalists and 3rd the third-place winner. 4th is the third-place loser. **5th to 8th** are the quarter-final losers, ranked by points per match over the phase, then goal difference, then goals scored.
4. **This ranking is France's "league" for U13 access** (Framework Art. 11.4.2). It sends teams to the YCL, YEL and YECL U13 by France's place in the access list.
5. **Promotion is separate:** every Interregional U12 group winner goes up to Elite 2 U13 whatever happens at Clairefontaine (Chapter 3).
6. Travel and three nights' hotel are paid by the FFF for non-professional clubs.

As Framework Art. 8.2, by the levels cohorts played in **2028-29**:

| Age group (2029-30) | Seeded from |
|---|---|
| **U18, U19** | The **U17 and U19 Nationaux** (4 groups of 14, 6 groups of 14), with the best into Elite 1 and Elite 2 |
| **U14–U17** | The **U14–U16 R1 tables**, with the best into Elite by points per match, then by the club's U17/U19 Nationaux level |

| **U9–U12** | Professional clubs by right; everyone else by their 2028-29 level |

## Appendix: research notes

- **The FFF's 13 metropolitan leagues** (*ligues régionales*) are the real ones: Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, Bourgogne-Franche-Comté, Bretagne, Centre-Val de Loire, Corse, Grand Est, Hauts-de-France, Méditerranée, Normandie, Nouvelle-Aquitaine, Occitanie, Paris Île-de-France, Pays de la Loire.
- **Today's national youth leagues:** **U19 Nationaux** (4 groups of 14) and **U17 Nationaux** (6 groups of 14), each followed by quarter-finals, semi-finals and a final. Every other youth age group plays regionally.
- **The Coupe Gambardella** is France's real national youth cup (U18), with its final played at the Stade de France before the senior Coupe de France final.
- **Overseas:** the real overseas leagues are affiliated to the FFF. New Caledonia and Tahiti have their own federations (OFC members) and are not in this annex.
- **Wednesday afternoons:** most French primary and many secondary schools have no lessons on Wednesday afternoon, which is when much youth football training happens.
- **Not verified:** the exact number of R1 teams per league and age group, which varies by league.
